Different Types of Ice on Commercial Ice Machines

Trade ice only · London commercial repair

Different types of ice in a London kitchen come from a commercial ice machine, not a domestic tray. Cube, gourmet, flake and nugget ice each use a different evaporator. When the bin is empty at Friday service, the fault is almost always the maker: scale on the plate, a failed pump, a dirty condenser or a water inlet that has stuck. Cube ice on catering ice machines

Full-dice and half-dice cubes are what most restaurants and hotels want in a glass. A cube machine freezes water on a vertical plate, then harvests with a hot-gas or harvest-valve cycle. Thin or cloudy cubes usually mean the water filter is spent or the inlet valve is not filling the trough. No harvest with a frozen plate is a harvest-valve, sensor or board fault. Gourmet and large clear cubes

Gourmet or “crescent” ice takes longer on the evaporator. Bars like the slow melt. The same commercial refrigeration circuit still has to reject heat. A gourmet maker that ices the bin slowly on a hot pass is often a blocked condenser on the back of the machine, not a “wrong ice type”. Clear the grille before you assume the model is undersized. If the cycle time has doubled after a few months of hard water, descale is due and the pump may already be noisy.

Flake and nugget ice

Flake ice is for fish, produce and display. Nugget or chewable ice is for QSR and healthcare. Both use an auger. When the gearbox groans or the bin fills with wet slush, stop the machine. Running an auger dry wrecks the evaporator. That is commercial ice machine repair London work, not a weekend clean. What type of ice does not change the repair

Scale, dirty condensers, failed pumps and low water pressure look the same whether the bin is meant to hold cubes or flake. Probe the incoming water. Photograph the data plate. Do not chip ice off an evaporator with a knife. If the machine is in a hot kitchen next to a fryer, the condenser will load with grease the same way a commercial fridge does. Choosing the right ice type for your venue

The ice your machine makes should match what you serve. A cocktail bar in Soho wants a slow-melt gourmet cube so the drink does not dilute before the customer finishes it. A fishmonger in Billingsgate needs flake ice that wraps around the catch without bruising it. A fast-service restaurant in Camden wants half-dice cubes because they fill a cup quickly and stack neatly in a bin. If the bin is always empty during service, the machine may be undersized for the venue, not the wrong ice type.

Half-dice cubes suit high-volume drinks service because they pack tighter and melt slower than full cubes in a packed bin. Full-dice cubes suit restaurants where ice sits in a glass for an hour and clarity matters. Gourmet crescent cubes suit bars that pour premium spirits over a single large cube. Nugget ice suits healthcare and QSR where the chewable texture is the point. Flake ice suits display, fish and produce where the ice moulds around the product.

How ice machine size affects breakdown frequency

An undersized machine runs longer cycles to fill the same bin, so the compressor, water pump and harvest valve all rack up hours faster than the manufacturer rated them. A machine that is too large for the site short-cycles and stalls. Both cause more breakdowns than a machine sized to the actual daily demand. If you are replacing a unit, tell us the bin size, the peak service hour, and how many covers or drinks you serve on a busy shift. We can tell you whether the model is the bottleneck.

Hard water is the other silent cost. Scale on the evaporator plate reduces heat transfer, so the harvest cycle stretches from minutes to tens of minutes. A descale every three to six months on London water keeps the cycle time stable. If the cycle time has doubled since the last service, the plate needs descaling before the compressor fails.

Scale, water filters and the bin

A blocked water filter on the inlet changes the harvest cycle before it changes the ice quality. If the trough is not filling, the plate freezes thin and the harvest drops early or not at all. Replace the inline filter every six months on London water, or quarterly if the site is in a hard water postcode. The bin itself also needs emptying and sanitising: mould in a wet bin is a food safety risk that an EHO will flag before the machine breaks. A clean bin with a dry base keeps the auger and the scoop safe.

When the machine is next to a fryer

An ice machine in a hot kitchen next to a fryer or combi oven loads the condenser with grease the same way a commercial fridge does. The condenser fins cake up, head pressure rises, and the compressor runs longer to reject the same heat. If the machine is air-cooled and sits in a corner with no make-up air, the condenser cannot breathe. Clean the fins weekly with a degreaser and keep the grille clear.
