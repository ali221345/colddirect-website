Commercial Fridge Repair Cost in London: 2025 Guide

Published 5 Sep 2025 · 9 min read

Owners ask for “the price of a fridge repair” as if it were an MOT. It is not. A torn gasket on a Polar under-counter is a different ticket from a seized compressor on a True two-door in Soho on a Saturday night. This 2025 guide sets out the bands London kitchens actually see: call-out, labour, and common parts. Figures are typical trade ranges, not a fixed menu. VAT, congestion, parking and access can move them.

Use the numbers to sanity-check a quote, not to argue an engineer into a loss. If two written quotes differ by a few hundred pounds, the cheaper one is often missing F-gas recovery, a second visit, or a genuine OEM part.

Average Callout Fee in North London (£89-£120)

Daytime, in-hours call-outs for a commercial cabinet in North London commonly land between £89 and £120. That usually covers travel in the local radius and the first slab of diagnosis time. It is not a promise that the fridge will be cold before the engineer leaves.

Out-of-hours, Sunday and bank-holiday rates sit higher. Expect a premium rather than the weekday band. Central postcodes add parking and congestion. A site with no loading bay and a fridge in a basement can turn a “local” job into a two-person carry.

Ask what the fee includes. Some companies deduct it from the repair if you authorise work on the same visit. Some do not. Get that in one sentence on the phone before you give the address.

A “free call-out” advert often hides the labour in a higher hourly rate or a minimum two-hour charge. Compare the total to get the fridge holding temperature again, not the headline.

Labour Cost Per Hour

Once diagnosis starts, labour in London for a refrigeration engineer is commonly billed in the region of £70 to £110 per hour in hours, more after 6pm. A simple gasket or controller swap may finish inside the first hour. A leak search with nitrogen, a vacuum and a recharge will not.

F-gas work is skilled labour with legal paperwork. Anyone offering to “top up the gas” without finding the leak is selling you a temporary cool-down and a repeat bill. Budget the leak search as its own line.

Two-person jobs (heavy doors, roof packs, awkward stairs) double the labour line even when the fault is simple. That is physics, not greed.

These are parts-only bands for common True, Foster, Williams, Polar and similar trade cabinets. A genuine Foster controller is not the same price as a generic thermostat from a catalogue. Glass-door gaskets and three-door counters sit at the top of the gasket band. Hermetic compressors on large uprights can exceed £900 before gas and labour.

Always photograph the data plate. Model and serial change the gasket drawing. Ordering “a Williams gasket” without the model is how you pay twice.

Refrigerant itself is a separate cost when the circuit is opened. The kilogram price moves with the gas type. Older cabinets on phased-down gases can surprise people who last repaired the unit in 2018.

What Makes Cost Go Up

Access, time of day, and the number of visits. A part that has to come from the manufacturer turns one visit into two. A leaking evaporator under a fully loaded counter means emptying, recovery, weld or replacement, vacuum, and recharge. That is a day, not a gasket.

Contamination after a compressor burnout adds flush and filter-drier work. Ignore that step and the new compressor dies. The “cheap compressor swap” then becomes two compressors.

Sites that never clean condensers pay in extra hours of diagnosis because every symptom looks like low gas. Five minutes with a coil brush on a maintenance contract is cheaper than a Saturday leak search that finds a packed condenser.

Repair vs Replace

A useful rule of thumb: if the quoted repair is more than about 50–60% of a like-for-like new cabinet, and the liner is rusted or the doors no longer seal even with a new gasket, replacement is often the adult decision. A five-year-old True or Williams with a dead fan is almost always a repair. A fifteen-year-old no-name counter with a flat compressor and a rotten base is a replacement conversation.

Lead time matters more than the last £80. If a new cabinet is two weeks away and you have a wedding menu on Saturday, paying for a compressor this week can still be rational. If you have a spare under-counter, you can wait for a proper replacement and stop pouring money into a dying box.

Ask the engineer for a written split: labour, parts, F-gas, and whether the cabinet is worth it. A good report lets you choose. A shrug and a round number does not.

Energy is the hidden line. A cabinet that runs 24 hours with a dirty condenser and a weak door seal can cost more in electricity over a year than the gasket and service you postponed. Factor that if you are comparing “live with it” against a repair.

None of these bands is a contract. Get a site quote. Move stock before the visit if the unit is already warm. Have the data plate photo ready. That is how London kitchens keep the bill in the band they expected.
