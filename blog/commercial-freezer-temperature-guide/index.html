Commercial Freezer Temperature Guide for Restaurants

Legal limits, HACCP bands and probe points · London

A commercial freezer in a London restaurant should hold minus 18 degrees Celsius or colder. That is not a recommendation — it is the legal requirement under UK food safety regulations for frozen storage. If the cabinet is running at minus 12, the product is still frozen but the safety margin is gone. This guide covers the temperatures your EHO expects, how to probe correctly, and when to call an engineer before the stock is lost. The legal temperature for commercial freezers

UK food safety law requires frozen food to be stored at minus 18 degrees or colder. The cabinet air temperature should hold between minus 18 and minus 22. If the cabinet drifts above minus 15, the product is entering the "partial thaw" zone: ice crystals grow, texture degrades, and the shelf life shortens. An EHO visit will flag a cabinet running above minus 15 even if the product still feels frozen.

Ice cream and gelato need colder storage — minus 22 to minus 25 — because partial thaw ruins the texture before the product reads "warm" on a probe. A freezer holding ice cream at minus 12 will produce a grainy scoop by the next morning. See ice cream machine repair London if the serving machine is also struggling.

How to probe a commercial freezer

Probe the core of the product, not the air between packs. The air temperature at the door can read 10 degrees warmer than the product at the back when the door opens during service. Push a probe between two packs or into the centre of a frozen block. Wait 30 seconds for the reading to stabilise. Record the temperature, the time, the product probed and your initials.

A calibrated digital probe is the only tool an EHO accepts. Infrared thermometers read the surface, not the core, and a surface reading on a frost-covered pack will read colder than the product inside. Keep a calibrated probe in the kitchen and check it against ice water (0 degrees) and boiling water (100 degrees) monthly.

HACCP temperature bands

Your HACCP plan should define four bands for the freezer:

  • Safe: minus 18 to minus 22. Product is in spec. Log and move on.
  • Caution: minus 15 to minus 18. The cabinet is drifting. Check the door seal, the condenser, and whether the cabinet was overstocked. Log the reading and re-probe in two hours. If it is still climbing, call an engineer.
  • Warning: minus 10 to minus 15. The cabinet is failing. Move high-risk product to a backup freezer if you have one. Call Cold Direct on 07983 759320. Do not wait until the end of service.
  • Danger: above minus 10. The product is at risk. Move stock immediately. Discard anything that has partially thawed and refrozen (ice crystals on the surface, soft centre). Call for emergency repair now.

What causes a freezer to warm up

The five causes we see most in London kitchens are the same as for fridges but the stakes are higher because a freezer takes longer to recover:

  • Dirty condenser. Grease and dust block the fins, head pressure rises, the compressor runs longer but cannot reject enough heat. Clean monthly.
  • Door seal failure. Warm moist air enters, the evaporator ices solid, and the cabinet cannot pull down. Airflow stops before the product warms.
  • Iced evaporator. If the defrost heater or timer has failed, ice builds on the coil until airflow stops. The cabinet reads warm even though the compressor is running.
  • Refrigerant leak. A slow leak drops the charge until the cabinet cannot hold minus 18. The compressor runs continuously and eventually trips on thermal.
  • Controller failure. The controller loses its set point after a power cut or a spike. The cabinet runs at minus 10 instead of minus 20 and the product slowly warms.

Walk-in freezer rooms

Walk-in freezer rooms hold minus 20 to minus 25. The same rules apply but the plant is bigger: door heaters, remote condensers, and evaporator fans in the ceiling. A walk-in that is running at minus 15 has a failing component. Check the door heater first — if the door is freezing shut, the heater tape has failed and warm air is leaking in through the seal. Check the remote condenser on the roof or in the plant room — if it is blocked with leaves or pigeon debris, the compressor cannot reject heat. See freezer room repair London for walk-in service.

What to do while you wait for an engineer

Keep the door closed. Every opening lets warm air in and pushes the cabinet further from recovery. Do not add stock to a warming cabinet. If you have a backup freezer or a hire cabinet, move high-risk product there. If the product is already above minus 10, probe each pack: anything soft to the touch has partially thawed and should be discarded or used immediately under your food safety policy. Do not refreeze partially thawed product — the texture and safety are already compromised.

If the cabinet is still running but drifting, do not switch it off. A running compressor at minus 15 is better than no compressor at all. Let it keep trying while you wait. Switching it off and on again does not fix a dirty condenser or a leak — it just resets the controller for ten minutes.

Chest freezers versus uprights

Chest freezers hold temperature longer during a fault because cold air sinks and stays in the chest when the lid opens. An upright freezer loses cold air every time the door opens — the cold air falls out onto the floor. If you are choosing between the two for a busy kitchen, a chest freezer is more resilient for storage, but an upright is easier to organise for service. Both need the same maintenance: condenser, door seal, and defrost cycle.

Chest freezers for ice cream should be set to minus 22 or colder. A common London fault is a chest freezer running at minus 15 after the lid seal flattens from years of use. Replace the lid gasket before the ice cream goes grainy.
