What F-Gas is trying to stop

Many catering systems still use HFC refrigerants with a high global-warming potential. The rules limit who can handle those gases, push leak checks on larger charges, and restrict topping up a leaking system as a habit. A “regas” without a leak repair is how the same hotel cold room fails every month and how gas is vented into the air. An unlicensed person adding gas from a cylinder bought online is not a workaround. It is the opposite of compliance.

Who may work on your commercial system

Leak testing, recovery, charging and breaking into the circuit are jobs for people with the right F-Gas company and personnel certification. Duty managers in takeaways and cafes should not pierce pipes or loosen Schrader caps. Your job is to notice oil stains, ice in stripes, hissing and rising temperatures, then book a certified commercial engineer. Ask to see competence if you are a group estates manager. Cold Direct attends commercial sites only.

Leak checks and records

Larger commercial systems and many cold rooms need leak checks on a schedule that depends on charge and gas type. Keep invoices, gas quantities and leak-test dates with the asset file. Hotels with several walk-ins should treat each system as its own record. Shops with a remote pack in a plant room are not the same as a self-contained Polar under-counter, but both can leak. If an EHO or insurer asks how you responded to a warm room, a folder of temperatures plus an F-Gas invoice is stronger than a verbal “we put gas in last year”.

What you should do when a cabinet or room warms

Do not keep adding refrigerant as a weekly habit. Isolate if it is safe, protect stock under your HACCP rules, and call. A commercial fridge or freezer that ices in a stripe, oils around a joint, or never holds set point after a recent “top-up” is a leak until proven otherwise. Walk-in doors that will not seal also waste energy and can look like a gas problem. Book commercial cold room repair London for rooms. Use the cabinet pillars for uprights and prep counters.

Older gases and replacement plant

High-GWP gases are being squeezed. A 15-year-old R404A pack in a restaurant may still be legal to service with conditions, but a major leak can make replacement the cheaper compliance path. We will not pretend a compressor change is always cheaper than a new cabinet. We will diagnose first. Pubs and hotels planning a refit should ask about lower-GWP options before the old plant fails on a Saturday.

Myths that cost businesses money

Myth: “It is only a small under-counter, F-Gas does not apply.” Handling the gas still needs competence. Myth: “The chef can add gas from a can.” That is how you buy a second leak and a legal problem.
