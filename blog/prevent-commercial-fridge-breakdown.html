How to Prevent Commercial Fridge Breakdown

Planned maintenance for trade kitchens · London

A commercial fridge breakdown at 10pm on a Saturday is not the time to find out the condenser was blocked. Most emergency call-outs we attend in London trace back to five things that planned maintenance catches before they fail. This guide is for duty managers, chefs and facilities leads who want to stop the Saturday night call and keep the EHO happy. If your cabinet has already failed, book commercial fridge repair London now and come back to this page after the fix.

Clean the condenser every month

The condenser is the black coil on the back or top of the cabinet. In a commercial kitchen it collects grease, flour and dust until the airflow drops and the head pressure climbs. A dirty condenser is still the number one cause of "warm fridge" calls we log. Brush or vacuum the fins monthly. If the fins are greasy, use a degreaser and a soft brush, then rinse. A condenser that cannot breathe forces the compressor to run longer, which shortens its life and raises your electricity bill at the same time.

If the cabinet is in a corner with no make-up air, move it or cut a vent. An air-cooled condenser needs at least 30cm of clearance on the intake side. A cabinet pushed against a wall with boxes stacked on the grille is the same as a blocked condenser. We see this on uprights in small prep kitchens more than anywhere else.

Check door seals every quarter

Torn or flattened gaskets let warm moist air into the cabinet. The evaporator ices, the display climbs, and the compressor runs continuously. Hold a piece of paper in the door seal: if it pulls out easily, the gasket is gone. Replace it before the ice builds up. A new gasket is cheaper than a compressor and faster than a Saturday call-out.

Door closers and hinges also fail. If the door does not close on its own from a 30-degree opening, the closer spring is worn. A door that swings open during service lets warm air in every time a chef reaches for a pan. Check the hinge pins and the closer body. If the door drops, the hinge is bent and the seal will never hold until the hinge is straightened or replaced.

Keep a temperature log

A written temperature log is the cheapest diagnostic tool you own. Probe the core of a product in the cabinet twice a day: at opening and at the end of service. Write the reading, the time and your initials. If the cabinet was holding 3 degrees on Tuesday and 7 degrees on Friday, the trend tells us the condenser is loading up or the refrigerant is leaking before the cabinet actually fails. A log also keeps your EHO records in order.

Digital data loggers are cheap now. A Bluetooth logger that records every 15 minutes and exports a PDF costs less than a single call-out. If the cabinet has a history of drifting, a logger will catch it before the bin is warm.

Do not overload the cabinet

A commercial fridge is rated for a specific load density. If shelves are stacked solid to the back wall, cold air cannot circulate. The product on the top shelf might be in spec while the bottom shelf is at 10 degrees. Leave a gap between product and the back wall, and do not block the airflow with trays or cardboard. A cabinet that is overstocked on a Friday night is the same as a blocked condenser by Sunday morning.

Hot food in a fridge is the other classic. Putting a tray of cooked food above 60 degrees into a cabinet will raise the air temperature for hours, ice the evaporator, and potentially put other product in the danger zone. Cool food to below 15 degrees before it goes in. Blast chill the batch if you have one — see blast chiller repair London if the chiller is also down.

Book a PPM service contract

Planned preventive maintenance (PPM) is a service visit where an engineer cleans the condenser, checks the refrigerant charge, inspects door seals, tests the controller, checks the drain and writes a report. A PPM visit every six months catches the slow faults before they become emergencies. The visit also gives you a service record for your EHO inspection and your insurer.

Cold Direct runs PPM contracts for restaurants, pubs, hotels and shops across Greater London. A contract costs less than two emergency call-outs and reduces the number of call-outs you need. If you already have a cabinet failing, book the repair first, then ask about a contract for the rest of the kitchen.

What a PPM visit covers

A standard visit includes: condenser clean and airflow check, evaporator inspection, door seal and closer test, refrigerant pressure and leak check, controller set-point verification, drain clearance, electrical safety check, and a written report with readings. If a part is wearing but has not failed, the engineer flags it on the report so you can budget the replacement before it breaks at midnight.

For walk-in cold rooms the visit also covers door heaters, panel seals, and the remote condenser on the roof or in the plant room. See cold room repair London if the walk-in is already climbing.

Brands and models we maintain

Foster, Williams, True, Gram, Polar, Blizzard, Tefcold, Lec, Liebherr and Empire uprights and under-counters are the most common cabinets in London kitchens. We also maintain Adexa and budget trade cabinets. The maintenance is the same regardless of brand: the condenser, the door, the controller and the charge are the four things that fail. Genuine parts are fitted where they match the data plate. Send a photo of the plate if you want us to check parts stock before the visit.

When to stop maintaining and call

If the cabinet is already above 8 degrees, the product is at risk and the compressor is running continuously, stop the maintenance checklist and call. A cabinet that has climbed to 10 degrees with the compressor hot is not a cleaning job — it is a repair. Move high-risk product to a working cabinet or a freezer if you have one.
