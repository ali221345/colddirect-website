Why ice appears on a trade freezer

Moisture plus a surface below freezing equals ice. In a commercial kitchen that moisture comes from door openings, wet boxes, steam from a pass and torn gaskets. A light frost film after Friday service is normal. A solid block over the coil, ice on the ceiling of the cabinet, or a door that will not close because of a ridge of ice is a fault. Staff often scrape ice and reopen the same cabinet an hour later. That cycle hides the cause and can puncture the liner. Power down, catch melt water, then work through the five causes below before you assume the compressor has failed.

Cause 1: Door seal and door heater

Close a sheet of paper in the door. If it slides out with no grip, kitchen air is leaking in all day. A torn gasket on a Williams, Foster, Polar or True catering freezer is the most common reason a commercial freezer ices up around the frame. Pubs and takeaways that slam doors during service wear the magnet strip first. Door-frame heaters that have failed leave a wet stripe that freezes into a wedge. Wipe grease off the rubber. If the magnet no longer pulls evenly, you need a replacement profile, not another cloth. Do not tape a domestic gasket onto a trade cabinet.

Cause 2: Defrost heater, timer or probe

Automatic defrost melts frost on a schedule. If the heater, drain heater or timer fails, frost becomes a block. The fans then throw ice chips onto product. Controllers that skip defrost after a power cut look like “it iced up overnight”. Photograph the error code. A commercial freezer icing up every morning after a clean coil is usually defrost, not “needs more gas”.

Cause 3: Blocked evaporator drain

Melt water has to leave the coil tray. Food debris, ice in the drain and a failed drain heater send water back into the fan. Ice then grows from the bottom of the liner. Hotels and restaurants that store open sauces in the freezer see this weekly. Clear only what you can see with the power isolated. Do not poke a screwdriver into a drain that sits next to a live heater.

Cause 4: Kitchen humidity and door discipline

A takeaway pass at 35°C with the freezer door wedged open while staff pick bags will ice any healthy cabinet. Cafes that load warm pastry, shops that restock from a wet delivery, and hotel banquet stores that stand open during plating all add kilos of moisture. Train the close-the-door habit. It will not fix a torn gasket, but it stops a good freezer looking like a failed one.

Cause 5: Refrigerant leak and iced suction

A leak can leave one patch of the coil arctic and the rest of the cabinet warm. Ice forms in a stripe, not a uniform film. Adding gas without a leak test is illegal for unlicensed people and usually fails again. That is F-Gas work for a commercial freezer engineer. Cold Direct diagnoses leaks on trade plant only. Safe first steps for the duty manager

Keep the door shut until you have a plan. Move high-risk frozen food to a second cabinet that is still at minus 18 if you have one. Probe product cores, not air at the door. Isolate power if you intend a full defrost. Catch water so it does not flood a kitchen floor. Do not chip ice. Do not run a hairdryer into a plastic liner. FAQ: icing causes

Why is my commercial freezer icing up?

Door seals, failed defrost, blocked drains, humidity from open doors, or a refrigerant leak. Ice is the symptom. The cause needs a trade diagnosis.

Is icing always a compressor fault?

No. Most icing we see in restaurants, pubs, takeaways, cafes, shops and hotels is air leaks or defrost, not a dead compressor.
