Glass-door merchandisers

True T-series, Williams, Foster, Polar and Blizzard glass-door bottles and food merchandisers live in cafés, gyms, hotel lobbies and back bars. Door heaters stop the glass sweating. If the heater fails, condensation looks like a “gas leak” to staff. Gaskets tear at the hinge. Self-closing hardware fails and the door stands ajar on a busy Saturday. Integral condensers sit behind a grille that fills with till rolls and cardboard. We pull the grille before we talk about refrigerant. A blocked condenser is still the most common London merchandiser call.

LED lighting and door switches matter. A door switch that does not stop the fan when the door is open can ice the coil. A lighting transformer that dumps heat into a small canopy lifts the top-shelf temperature. Those are display-fridge faults, not kitchen-cabinet faults.

Open decks and air curtains

An open display fridge has no door. It lives or dies on the air curtain. Stock past the load line, a trolley parked in the throw, a missing honeycomb or a failed fan will warm the front row first. Night blinds that stay up overnight wreck the next day’s recovery. We measure the curtain. We do not guess from the door-less “it feels cold when I put my hand in”. Hands are not HACCP probes.

Sweating glass is not always a leak

  • Failed door heater: condensation on the pane, customers think the fridge is “broken”.
  • Torn gasket: moist air in, coil ices, drinks warm by afternoon.
  • Cardboard on the rear grille: integral condenser cooks, compressor short-cycles.
