What a trade cabinet fridge actually is

Chefs say “the fridge” and mean a 600 mm or 700 mm Gastronorm cabinet with removable racks, a digital controller, a door heater and a condenser stuffed behind a grille at the top or bottom. Foster EcoPro, Williams Jade, True T-series kitchen models, Gram Compact, Polar G-series and Blizzard under-counters all live in that family. They are designed to recover after the door has been open on a busy pass. They are not designed to survive a week of flour, oil mist and cardboard stacked against the coil. That is why cabinet fridge repair London is a weekly diary item for us, not a rare call.

Under-counter cabinets fail for a different reason to tall uprights. The compressor sits in a tiny compartment next to a dishwasher steam plume. Head pressure rises, oil stays in the condenser, and the well warms even while the compressor sounds busy. Tall cabinets ice the evaporator when a latch is left off or a gasket has a split at the hinge. Pass-through cabinets ice both sides if staff prop both doors during service. We treat those as three different jobs even though Google lumps them under one phrase.

Faults we log on cabinet fridge jobs

The Saturday classic is a blocked condenser. Grease and pastry dust cake the fins, the fan still spins, and the cabinet cannot hold 1 to 4 °C. An iced evaporator after a door left on the latch starves airflow. Torn gaskets let moist air in until the coil ices and the display climbs. Evaporator fans seize. Controllers lose their set point after a power cut. Drain trays overflow onto the floor and into the electrics. Compressors weaken and then trip. Low refrigerant after a vibration leak on a liquid line looks like “it just got warm” until we weigh the charge.

If a trade cabinet is running but product sits at 10 °C, do not keep adding stock. We measure air on and off the coil before we talk parts. Staff sometimes stack boxes against the grille; we will say so if that is the whole job. A cabinet that hunts between 2 and 12 °C is often a control, drain or door-seal issue, not a dead compressor. A silent cabinet with a warm evaporator and a hot compressor shell is a different job. We write both air and product temperatures for your HACCP sheet.

Why a catering cabinet fridge stops holding temperature

  • Basement plant cupboards with no make-up air cook an integral condenser; the fridge short-cycles and oil stays in the coil so capacity drops even after you reset the switch.
  • Night prep leaves doors open on a two-door under-counter; by breakfast the evaporator is a solid block and the digital alarm has been muted.
  • Vibration from a nearby tube line loosens a flare on a remote liquid line; a slow leak empties the charge over a week while the display still looks fine at opening.

How we diagnose a warm cabinet without guessing

We probe air on, air off and a packed tray. We check superheat if the charge looks light. We pull the grille and photograph the condenser before we talk about gas. That sequence is how cabinet fridge repair stays a first-visit fix instead of a recharge that fails again on Friday.
