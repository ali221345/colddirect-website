How dairy cabinets differ from ordinary display fridges

Dairy lines are high-risk and short-dated. A warm top shelf is not a merchandising inconvenience; it is a food-safety incident. Serve-over cheese counters run a gravity well with a glass sneeze screen. Integral multidecks run an air curtain that collapses if someone parks a milk crate in the return. Remote cases on a pack in the back room fail when the pack is short of charge or a solenoid sticks. Spilt milk in the well is not only a smell: it blocks the drain, ices the coil and trips the case. We treat hygiene and refrigeration as the same visit.

Night blinds that stay up, or night blinds that jam and tear, change the load on the curtain. A case that holds overnight with the blind down can fail by 10:00 with the blind up and the aisle doors open. That is a London shop pattern, not a compressor mystery.

Faults we log

Iced evaporators after a doorless deck has been loaded beyond the load line. Blocked honeycomb. Failed evaporator fans. Dirty integral condensers behind a grille stuffed with price tickets. Torn night blinds. Failed controllers after a power cut. Leaking bottle wells. Remote pack issues that look like “the dairy aisle is warm” while the produce case on the same pack still looks fine. We map the pack before we recover refrigerant. We will not recharge a leaking system and drive away.

If product at the rear of the well is 3 °C and the front rail is 11 °C, say so when you call. That split is an air-curtain story. If the whole well is 12 °C and the pack is silent, that is a plant story. The van stocking changes with those two sentences.

What to do before we arrive

Pull high-risk dairy into a working cabinet if you have one. Keep remaining stock behind the load line. Put the night blind down if the case is still running. Do not pour hot water into an iced well. Photograph the data plate and the controller alarm. Clear access to the pack or the integral grille. Have a member of staff who can isolate the case without killing the whole shop lighting circuit.

Why the front rail runs warm

  • Stock past the load line: the curtain cannot close, the front packs sit in aisle air.
  • Honeycomb blocked with price tickets and dust: throw is uneven, top shelf climbs first.
  • Spilt milk in the well: drain ices, coil ices, then the whole case hunts.
