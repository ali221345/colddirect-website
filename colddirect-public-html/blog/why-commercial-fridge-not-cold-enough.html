Why Is My Commercial Fridge Not Cold Enough?

Published 7 Sep 2026 · 8 min read

A commercial fridge not cold enough is a food-safety problem first. Soft dairy, warm cooked rice and a display that sits at 10 °C instead of 3 °C mean you are already taking a risk. This guide is for trade uprights, prep counters and under-counters in London kitchens. It is not a DIY recharge manual. It tells a duty manager what to check in the next twenty minutes, and when to stop and book commercial fridge repair London.

What “not cold enough” actually means

Staff often say a commercial fridge not cold enough when the fans are still spinning. That is useful. A silent cabinet with a warm evaporator is a different fault from a noisy cabinet that cannot pull down. Probe product in the middle of a packed tray, not the air at the door. 4 °C on the display and 11 °C in chicken is a food-safety issue, not a “wait until morning” issue.

Most catering fridges are designed to hold 1 to 4 °C in a kitchen that is already hot. If the room is 32 degrees and the condenser is caked in flour, the cabinet will run all day and still look like a commercial fridge not cold enough. Clearing the grille sometimes restores the last few degrees. Sometimes it does not.

Five checks before you call

1. Condenser and airflow

Pull the kick plate if you can do it safely. A grey felt of grease on the condenser coil is the Saturday classic. Vacuum the lint if the unit is isolated. Do not hose a live electrical tray. Leave 10 cm of air behind the cabinet. Boxes stacked against the grille are enough to stop a healthy compressor from rejecting heat.

2. Door seal

Close a sheet of paper in the door. If it slides out with no grip, moist kitchen air is leaking in all day. Wipe grease off the rubber. If the magnet no longer pulls evenly, you need a replacement profile, not another wipe. A torn gasket is a common reason a commercial fridge not cold enough still “sounds fine”.

3. Ice on the evaporator

Open the inner liner. A light frost film after Friday service can be normal. A solid block over the fan blades is not. Ice is insulation. The coil cannot take heat out of the air, so the thermostat keeps calling and the food warms. Power down and let ice melt into trays. If ice returns the next day, the gasket, drain or defrost system has failed.

4. Controller and set point

After a power cut, some Dixell and Carel controllers boot to a warmer set point. Staff then report a commercial fridge not cold enough when the board is simply asking for 8 °C. Photograph the display and the data plate. If the set point is 2 °C and the coil is clear, the fault is further in: sensor, relay or compressor.

5. Compressor and charge

A compressor that is hot, cycling every minute, or silent with a warm suction line is not a staff job. Low refrigerant after a leak looks like “it just got warm”. Adding gas without a leak test is how the same cabinet fails again next week. That work needs an F-Gas engineer.

What not to do

Do not keep adding warm prep into a cabinet that is already above 8 °C. Do not cover the shelves with cling film so air cannot move. Do not assume a second thermostat from a parts website will match your controller. If you need attendance tonight, use commercial fridge repair London rather than waiting for a weekday slot.

When the problem is a walk-in, not a cabinet

A walk-in that is a commercial fridge not cold enough often has a different cause: a failed evaporator fan, a door left on the latch, or a condenser on a hot roof. Cabinets and rooms share refrigerant logic but not the same page. Walk-in plant is booked on cold room repair. Frozen cabinets sit on commercial freezer repair London.

Food you can still save

Keep the door shut until you have a plan. Move high-risk items if you have a second fridge that is still at 1 to 4 °C. Probe cores. Follow your own HACCP rules. An engineer can tell you whether the plant will recover in an hour or whether you should hire a cabinet. They cannot legally tell you to sell food that has been in the danger zone.
