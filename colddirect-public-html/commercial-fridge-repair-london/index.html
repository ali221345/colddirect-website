Common Commercial Fridge Faults

The faults we log on these jobs are a short list. A dirty condenser in a hot kitchen is still the Saturday classic: grease and flour cake the coil, head pressure rises, and the cabinet cannot hold 1 to 4 °C. An iced evaporator after a door left on the latch starves airflow. Torn gaskets let moist air in until the coil ices and the display climbs. Evaporator fans seize. Controllers lose their set point after a power cut. Compressors weaken and then trip. Low refrigerant after a leak looks like “it just got warm” until we weigh the charge.

If a trade cabinet is running but product sits at 10 °C, do not keep adding stock. We measure air on and off the coil before we talk parts. Why Your Commercial Fridge Stopped Working?

  • Basement plant rooms with no make-up air cook an integral condenser; the fridge short-cycles and oil stays in the coil so capacity drops even after you “reset” the switch.
  • Night deliveries leave doors open on a glass merchandiser; by breakfast the evaporator is a solid block and the curtain on an open deck has collapsed.
  • Vibration on a split liquid line from street traffic or a nearby tube line loosens a joint; a slow leak empties the charge over a week while the display still looks fine at opening.
