Why walk-in freezers ice harder than chill rooms

Every door opening dumps moist kitchen air onto a coil that is far colder than a fridge evaporator. If the door heater has failed, the gasket freezes to the frame. Staff then lever the handle and tear the seal, which dumps more moisture. If the defrost clock or sensors fail, ice becomes a solid block around the fan. The fan then throws nothing. Product at the door still feels hard while the back of the room climbs. Floor heaters that have failed let ice grow under pallets until the door will not close. None of that is a domestic chest-freezer story.

Low refrigerant at freezer temperatures looks like a room that “takes forever” after a delivery. We leak-test. We do not add gas and leave. Oil logging after a short cycle in cold weather is another London pattern: the compressor sounds busy and the room still will not pull down. That needs a proper diagnosis, not a reset of the isolator.

First-visit work

We measure air and product. We inspect the evaporator, drain, heaters, door frame and outdoor unit. We look at how stock is stacked. Pallets against the coil are a common own-goal. We defrost safely when ice is the immediate block. We quote before a compressor swap. We write the readings you need if Environmental Health asks why the graph spiked.

Before we arrive: keep the door shut. Stop adding warm trays. Photograph the controller and the condensing-unit plate. Clear roof or cage access. Do not attack ice with a knife or a heat gun pointed at foam panels. That is how you turn a repair into a panel replacement.
