Cellar warming unevenly despite a working cooling unit?

A cooler can be mechanically perfect and still lose the room. Heat walks in through an uninsulated ceiling under a warm kitchen, a perished door seal or a delivery door left open; moisture follows, and you get condensation, mould on casks and a cooler running flat out for nothing.

We survey the room as a room: dimensions and heat load, insulation and air leaks, airflow and humidity at several points, then the cooling unit itself. You get the findings in plain English and a fixed quote for whatever actually needs doing. Ring 0203 952 4822 or 07983 759320.

What a whole-cellar survey actually involves

  • Heat load: room size, what is stored (cask and keg give off heat of their own), motors and lights, and how often the door opens on delivery days.
  • Fabric: insulation condition, door seals, and the ceiling under warm kitchens — the usual route heat takes into a London basement.
  • Air and moisture: temperature readings at several points, condensation trails, and where the cooler's discharge air actually goes.
  • The unit: fans, coil condition, refrigerant sighting, and whether the cooler is even sized for the room it is fighting.

What we do not do is structural or damp-proofing building work. If the survey points that way, we will say so and tell you the trade you need. Cooling-side repairs are ours.

What to check before you call an engineer

Three things account for a surprising share of warm-cellar calls. First, the condenser grille on the cooling unit — a felted mat of dust means the unit cannot dump its heat. Second, the cellar door: if the seal has gone or the door is wedged open for deliveries, you are paying to cool the street. Third, the set point — controllers get knocked, and a pub cellar wants 11–13°C for cask and keg.

If all three are fine and the room is still climbing or uneven, ring 0203 952 4822 or 07983 759320. Tell us the cellar temperature, what the unit is doing — running constantly, short-cycling, or dead — and your postcode.

What temperature should a pub beer cellar be kept at in London?

Standard UK pub cellars must hold a steady 11–13°C (52–55°F) for cask conditioned ales and keg storage, while remote glycol/ice-bank line chillers pull draught lager down to 2–6°C at the tap.
