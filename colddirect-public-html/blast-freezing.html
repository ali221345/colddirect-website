How a blast freeze cycle is supposed to work

A probe goes into the slowest-cooling pack. The controller runs high fan speed and a low evaporator temperature until the core hits the set point, often minus 18. The cabinet then holds or beeps. If the probe is clipped to a rack instead of buried in product, the cycle ends while the centre of a lasagne is still warm. That is an operator fault we will name. If the probe is correct and the cycle still times out, we look at ice on the coil, a weak charge, a failing compressor, a blocked condenser or a fan that cannot throw air through a overloaded trolley.

Overloading is the London hotel pattern. A trolley packed like a holding freezer will not blast. Air needs lanes. We will say so if the plant is healthy and the load is the problem. That conversation saves you a compressor you did not need.

Faults we see on blast freezing plant

Ice-locked evaporators after staff run back-to-back cycles with no defrost. Condensers stuffed in a basement with no make-up air. Controllers that lose the probe and run forever. Door gaskets torn because trolleys hit the frame. Drain trays that freeze and dump water into the electrics. Refrigerant leaks on vibrating discharge lines. Compressors that overheat because oil has logged in a cold evaporator after a short cycle. None of those are domestic faults. None of them are fixed by “adding gas” without a test.

What to do while we travel

Stop adding trays. If product is still safe to chill in a working walk-in, move it under your own food-safety policy. Do not leave a half-finished blast load in a dead cabinet overnight and hope. Photograph the alarm, the probe position and the data plate. Clear access to the condenser. Have the HACCP probe reading ready so we are not guessing from the door display.

Cycle will not reach minus 18

  • Probe in the airflow instead of the product: cycle ends, core is still warm.
  • Coil iced from back-to-back cycles: air cannot pass, capacity collapses.
  • Basement condenser with no air: high pressure, short cycling, oil in the wrong place.

Blast freezing versus cold storage

Cold storage holds. Blast freezing pulls down. If you searched the wrong phrase, use cold storage for holding rooms. If the machine is an ice-cream freezer, that is still commercial refrigeration but a different evaporator jacket. See the ice cream machine page from the related links on this site.
