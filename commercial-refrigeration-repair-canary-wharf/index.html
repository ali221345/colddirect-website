Technical diagnostics our Canary Wharf engineers run first

Every Canary Wharf call-out starts at the plant room, on the roof or inside the controller — not with a guess. Our F-Gas certified engineers work on walk-in cold rooms, multideck runs, blast chillers and VRF-linked systems across the E14 estate, and we fault-find in a fixed order so facilities teams get a priced repair decision the same visit. These are the controller fault codes we see most around Canary Wharf:

Controller / code What it means on site First action we take
Carel E2 / E3 High or low temperature alarm — cabinet drifting out of range Check probe position, fan operation and condenser airflow before touching gas
Carel E4 / E5 Defrost or probe fault — ice build-up on the evaporator Force a defrost cycle, test heaters, verify the termination probe
Dixell XR06 / XR03 Parameter or sensor error after a power cut Re-read sensors against a calibrated thermometer, restore set points
Eliwell IDPlus AL1/AL2 Generic alarm logged overnight Pull the alarm history — it usually records the hour the plant started failing

Low refrigerant after a pinhole leak is the single most common root cause we find in the estate's rooftop condensers and long pipe runs. We pressure-test with trace gas, repair the leak, vacuum the circuit and recharge by weight — a legal F-Gas process with records your estate compliance file expects, not a top-up. Compressor electricals get a full check on every Canary Wharf job: start capacitor, run current against the data plate, and winding resistance across all windings. If a compressor is tight or the valves are passing, we quote a replacement with recovery and reclamation paperwork, and we can plan the swap into a service window so catering continuity is never at risk.
